# A family estimate, step by step

**All prices, product labels and dose counts below are invented for arithmetic practice.** They are not pharmacy quotes or dose recommendations. Confirm each person's actual product and remaining doses with their provider.

This example is included in the workbook's **Example** sheet.

| Input | Fictional option A | Fictional option B | Fictional option C |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Person 1, one remaining dose | $25 × 1 = $25 | $30 × 1 = $30 | $20 × 1 = $20 |
| Person 2, two remaining doses | $30 × 2 = $60 | $35 × 2 = $70 | $25 × 2 = $50 |
| Dose subtotal | $85 | $100 | $70 |
| Total extra fees | $0 | $10 | Unknown |
| Immediate discount | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Travel | Included: $14 | Included: $8 | Excluded |
| Estimated total | **$99** | **$118** | **Add fees** |

Option A's full calculation is:

```text
Person 1: $25.00 × 1 = $25.00
Person 2: $30.00 × 2 = $60.00
Dose subtotal:          $85.00
Extra fees:             +$0.00
Immediate discount:     −$0.00
Travel, counted once:  +$14.00
Estimated total:        $99.00
```

Option C has a $70 dose subtotal, but its extra fees are unknown and it excludes travel. Its subtotal is not a complete bill and cannot establish a cheaper option. Fill in the missing fee information and align the travel basis before comparing totals. Product and eligibility differences still need provider confirmation.

Try changing Option A's second unit price from $30 to $32. The two-dose line becomes $64, and the household estimate becomes $103. Change it back to restore the example.
